Medical Background
A 34-year-old male was prescribed amoxicillin for a suspected bacterial respiratory tract infection.
The patient started amoxicillin 500 mg orally three times daily on 01 September 2026.
Paracetamol 500 mg orally, as needed for fever, was also reported as a concomitant medication.
Approximately 36 hours after starting amoxicillin, the patient developed an itchy red skin rash over the chest and arms. The rash subsequently spread to the back and legs, accompanied by mild facial itching.
Amoxicillin was discontinued following medical evaluation. The patient's symptoms subsequently improved, and he recovered without further reported complications.

Narrative for the ICSR
A 34-year-old male received amoxicillin 500 mg orally three times daily for a suspected bacterial respiratory tract infection beginning on 01 September 2026. Approximately 36 hours after treatment initiation, on 02 September 2026, the patient developed an itchy red skin rash over the chest and arms, which subsequently spread to the back and legs. Mild facial itching was also reported.

Amoxicillin was discontinued on 03 September 2026 following medical evaluation. The patient's symptoms significantly improved by 04 September 2026, and he subsequently recovered without further reported complications.

The available information suggests a possible relationship between amoxicillin and the reported cutaneous reaction. Rechallenge was not performed. No serious outcome was reported in this fictional scenario.
Causality Assessment

Causality assessment evaluates the likelihood that the suspected medication caused the reported adverse reaction.

Overall Causality Classification
WHO-UMC-style assessment: Possible

Justification

The timing of the reaction and improvement after drug withdrawal support an association with amoxicillin. However, alternative causes have not been adequately excluded, and rechallenge information is unavailable.

Therefore, a conservative classification of Possible is appropriate for this illustrative case.

The classification is provisional and could change if additional clinical information became available.
